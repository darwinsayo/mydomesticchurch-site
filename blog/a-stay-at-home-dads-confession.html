A stay-at-home dad's confession

Why I started the My Domestic Church app

November 5, 2026 · Darwin Sayo

I thought I had it handled

I'm a stay-at-home dad. My wife and I decided one of us would stay home with the kids. I just never thought it would be me. Still, I figured I had the organized part covered. Before I was married, I managed information systems. Keeping things organized was my job.

It turns out a house full of children doesn't run like an information system. I wasn't nearly as organized as I thought I was.

When the routine broke

I chose our curriculum, and with our first children it worked well. We had a rhythm: lessons, chores, prayers, meals, all more or less in their place. Even then, we tried quite a few chore charts and chore systems.

Then life happened, the way it does. The family grew. We moved, more than once. And then we had a house fire. Thank God, everyone got out safe. But we lost everything. Overnight we were getting used to new clothes and living without the things that were familiar and well loved. We were starting from scratch. Watching my kids go through that was gut-wrenching, and in a way it was harder because they were too good to show it. Rebuilding a house is one thing. Rebuilding a routine, and a sense of home, is another. For months we were just getting through the day. School became whatever we could manage, and nobody was keeping track.

Each of those things on its own we could have weathered. Together, they knocked the routine apart. The schedule lived in my head, on scraps of paper, various apps on my phone, and on a calendar nobody looked at. Things slipped, quietly at first.

What it cost us

The kids fell behind. Not all at once, and not in a way you notice day to day, but it added up. The hardest moment was watching our eldest struggle in college, and knowing some of that traced back to the gaps at home.

That one stung. As a father, you want to hand your children a good foundation. I had to admit that the problem wasn't my kids, and it wasn't the curriculum. It was that our household had no system holding it all together.

Everything in one place

So I built something for us. I'm a photographer, not a programmer, but I've done a fair bit of web work over the years, and I knew what we needed: one place for the whole house. The day's schedule, school lessons for each child, chores, meals, the family calendar, and our prayers. And gentle reminders on our phones, plus a clear view of what's next, so nothing depended on me remembering it all.

The change came faster than I expected. Within a week, the kids started catching up. Everyone could see what the day held. The little things stopped falling through the cracks, and the house felt calmer. One day I was wondering where the older kids were and found them all in our pop-up trailer studying right on time.

Why I'm sharing it

I'm sharing the My Domestic Church app because I know we're not the only family this has happened to. Maybe you've had your own fire, your own move, your own new baby that turned the year upside down. If you're trying to catch up, I hope this helps you get there.

And if your home already runs well, maybe it can help you polish what you have. I have real respect for the moms with a binder, a printer, and a system that works. This isn't meant to replace what you already have. It's one more tool in the toolbox. Use what serves your family, print what you like, and keep the rest on your phone.

I learned that the answer wasn't to put everything on a screen. Some things just work better on paper. A daily schedule taped to the fridge, a chore card in a child's hand, a family board everyone walks past. So the app prints those, and the phone handles the rest. Analog and digital, working together, each doing what it does best.

The family is the domestic church. Our homes are where our children first learn to pray, to work, and to love one another. This is just one small tool to help keep that home in order.

God bless,
Darwin
